# Political risk and expropriation scenarios

Keywords: country risk expropriation break even probability annual hazard OGC High on the Hog KrugerWales local debt joint venture insurance.

[Main index](INDEX.md) | [Question bank](QUESTIONS.md) | [Exam reuse map](PATTERNS.md)

Checked supplementary reference, not an official marking scheme. Original course sources and current official corrections take precedence.


Identify the event, date, probability and cash flows lost. Expropriation is one form of political risk; taxes, controls, ownership rules and political disruption can also affect cash flows. Macro risk affects many firms; micro risk a particular firm/industry.

For one mutually exclusive event with probability $p$:

$$NPV(p)=(1-p)NPV_{no}+pNPV_{ex},\qquad
p^*=\frac{NPV_{no}}{NPV_{no}-NPV_{ex}}.$$

The second expression applies when the denominator is positive; state feasibility $p\in[0,1]$. Reject when expected NPV is negative; at zero the firm is indifferent. For an annual independent hazard and no recovery, survival to year $t$ is $(1-p)^t$ instead. A one-off salvage seizure is not an annual hazard and does not automatically destroy all operating cash flow.

For High on the Hog, the source specifies the joint states: 80% no change, 10% devaluation without expropriation, 10% devaluation with expropriation. Then expected CF is $0.8(50)+0.1(37.5)+0.1(0)=43.75$m. Do not multiply these state probabilities again as if independent. For genuinely conditional probabilities, construct the corresponding joint probabilities first.

Mitigation: local debt (sharing exposure with local lenders), local/international joint ventures or consortia, diversification, conservative cash-flow/scenario appraisal and political-risk insurance. Explain the channel and residual risk. Do not infer zero political risk from a high promised nominal yield.
