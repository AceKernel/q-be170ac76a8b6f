# Corporate governance, investor protection and LLSV

Corporate governance addresses conflicts between investors, managers and controlling shareholders. Distinguish **legal rights**, **enforcement**, and actual ownership/control; a promise in law is not automatically effective protection.

| Mechanism | Course application |
|---|---|
| Board, compensation, blockholders | Monitoring/alignment; incentives can also create manipulation or entrenchment |
| Takeovers and courts | External discipline and enforceable investor remedies |
| Dual-class shares, pyramids, cross-holdings | Control can exceed the investor's cash-flow stake |
| Cross-listing/bonding | Stronger disclosure and investor protection can reduce expropriation discounts; compliance/private-benefit costs remain |

LLSV (1998) examines historical legal rules/enforcement across 49 countries. Its original results generally associate common-law origin with stronger investor rights and French civil-law origin with weaker rights. **Do not equate the rights ranking with the enforcement ranking**: Scandinavian/German civil-law countries score highly on enforcement. Weak minority protection can discourage outside equity and lead to concentrated ownership as a monitoring substitute, while creating its own minority-expropriation risk.

The paper's antidirector index sums **six** rights (proxy by mail, no blocked shares, cumulative voting/proportional representation, oppressed-minority remedy, pre-emption, low threshold to call a meeting). **One-share-one-vote is a separate measure**, although discussed alongside them in the lecture. Creditor rights concern secured creditors, collateral/automatic stays, priority, reorganisation consent and management replacement.

Tutorial 11 Q7 uses the **median** top-three ownership values, US12% and Mexico67%; the paper also reports means US20% and Mexico64%. Do not confuse these statistics. Tutorial Q8 compares Italy and the UK: rights1 vs5; top-three ownership58% vs19%; market cap/GDP71% vs248%; listed firms247 vs2292. Explain the investor-protection/external-finance mechanism, not just the numbers.

**Required qualification:** Lecture 11 p38 discusses Spamann's corrected antidirector index and challenges to the original conclusions. Present the original LLSV findings as that study's findings, not settled universal causal law or current country rankings.
