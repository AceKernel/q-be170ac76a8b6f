# Cost of capital, segmentation and cross-listing

Use market-value weights and match cash-flow currency, risk and claimholder:

$$WACC=\frac{E}{D+E}R_E+\frac{D}{D+E}R_D(1-\tau),\qquad
R_E=R_f+\beta\bigl(\mathbb E[R_M]-R_f\bigr),$$
$$\beta=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(R_i,R_M)}{\operatorname{Var}(R_M)}
=\rho_{iM}\frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_M}.$$

Do not tax an already after-tax debt rate again. If the market risk premium is supplied, use it directly rather than subtracting $R_f$ twice. Domestic CAPM uses the domestic market; ICAPM the world market. Choose based on market integration and the marginal investor, not the firm's name or headquarters alone.

Under the tutorial's standard unlevering assumptions,

$$\beta_U=\frac{\beta_L}{1+(1-\tau)D/E}.$$

Tutorial 9 capital Q1: $D/E=1/2$, after-tax $R_D=7\%$, project $R_E=12\%$ gives project $WACC=(2/3)12\%+(1/3)7\%=10.3333\%$, not the parent's 15%. With $\beta_L=1.21$, $\tau=40\%$, $\beta_U=1.21/1.3=0.930769$.

Segmentation can arise from information barriers, illiquidity, transaction costs, capital controls and political risk. A broader investor base/cross-listing can improve liquidity, disclosure, recognition and diversification, lowering required returns and raising valuation; weigh compliance costs and loss of private control benefits. For Canva, explain the relevant technology-investor/analyst depth and valuation/cost-of-capital mechanism rather than listing generic advantages without context.

Lecture 10 distinguishes additive and beta-scaled country-risk adjustments. Under its relative-volatility method,

$$CRP=(\text{same-currency sovereign default spread})
\frac{\sigma_{equity}}{\sigma_{country\ bond}}.$$

Match the chosen model, benchmark and premium; do not combine incompatible CAPMs or add the same country risk twice.
