# Balance of payments and exchange-rate regimes

Use the course's **cash-flow/debit-credit convention**, not a different sign convention from an external statistical database. A payment to foreigners is a debit; a receipt from foreigners is a credit. Every transaction has an offsetting entry. Distinguish an acquisition of an asset from income earned on that asset.

| Account | Typical items |
|---|---|
| Current account | Goods, services, primary income, secondary income/transfers |
| Financial account | Direct investment, portfolio investment, other financial claims |
| Capital account | Capital transfers and specified non-produced/non-financial assets |
| Reserves and errors | Official settlement/reserve changes; statistical discrepancy |

The course sometimes uses 'capital account' broadly for capital/financial flows; preserve the question's definition. 'Overall BOP surplus/deficit' excludes its official financing counterpart; the complete double-entry accounts balance.

$$CA=Y-(C+I+G)=S_{\mathrm{national}}-I.$$

In the simplified model used for the IMF question, $CA=X-M$. A fall in government spending raises public/national saving and improves CA, **other things equal**. It is not a universal claim that any observed fiscal cut mechanically causes the same CA change. Trade balance alone excludes services, income and transfers.

The impossible trinity: a country cannot simultaneously maintain a fixed exchange rate, unrestricted capital mobility and independent monetary policy. In fixed-rate intervention, trace the currency bought/sold and the corresponding reserve change. In floating-rate diagrams, label which currency is priced, then shift its supply or demand.
