2. What is the major difference in the obligation of one with a long position in a futures

   (or forward) contract in comparison to an options contract? (*)


A futures (or forward) contract is a vehicle for buying or selling a stated amount of foreign

exchange at a stated price per unit at a specified time in the future. If the contract is held long

till the delivery date, the holder pays the effective contractual futures (or forward) price,

regardless of whether it is an advantageous price in comparison to the underlying spot price.

The options contract provides the holder with the right and not the obligation to buy/sell the

underlying asset at a specified price. This allows the holder to exercise the option if the spot

price is more favorable than the exercise price. Therefore, doesn’t have to exercise the option

if it is to his disadvantage and the only loss incurred is the premium.
