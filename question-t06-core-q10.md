10.  Airbus, a European company, sold an aircraft to Rex Airlines of Australia. Airbus
     have billed Rex $300 million, payable in six months. Airbus is concerned with the
     euro proceeds from international sales and would like to minimise exchange rate
      risk. The current spot exchange rate is $1.05/€ and the six-month forward exchange
     rate is $1.10/€. Airbus can buy a six-month put option on Australian dollars with a
      strike price of €0.95/$ for a premium of €0.025 per dollar. Currently the six-month
      interest rate is 5% per annum in the euro zone and 6.0% per annum in Australia.

     Airbus regards the current forward exchange rate is an unbiased predictor of the
     future spot exchange rate. If Airbus decides to hedge using put options on Australian
      dollars, what would be the “expected” Euro proceeds from the sale to Rex Airlines?

                                                        2024 exam question (*)



      In 6 months, the S will be $1.10/€ (or € 0.909/$) (i.e., today’s forward rate)


      In that case, Airbus will exercise the right to sell $ at 0.95 (as opposed to 0.909).

     The expected proceeds would be $300m × € 0.95/$ = € 285m.



    Need to subtract the premium paid today:

                            €0.025 × 300m = € 7.5 million

     Future value of premium:

                         € 7.5 million × (1+0.025) = € 7,687,500



     Total proceeds in 3 months:

                         € 285m - € 7,687,500 = € 277,312,500
